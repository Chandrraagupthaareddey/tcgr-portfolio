04.0 Case study

OIP — an Operational Intelligence
Platform, built from zero.

An AI-augmented operations platform for a payments on-call team. Designed, built and run by one engineer in six months — about two dozen repositories, a quarter-million lines, and a self-hosted triage model tuned from 30% to 75% in one pilot.

Design rules the platform never breaks
04.1 Architecture

Signals in, governed decisions out.

Simplified. Internal tool names are generalised; vendor names (Slack, Grafana, incident.io, Ollama, Trino) are real.

04.2 Cost-ordered inference

Spend the cheapest capable step first — and never let config reorder it.

Eligibility and health are tracked separately: a healthy model never gets promoted just because it is up. Models move through EXPERIMENTAL → SHADOW → HUMAN_REVIEW → QUALIFIED → APPROVED_PRIMARY, each step needing a named human and at least 30 adjudicated samples, with a regression gate of ±0.05 against the recorded baseline.

04.3 Measured, not claimed

A self-hosted model went from 30% to 75% agreement with human triage in a three-month pilot.

Held-out rows from a 7-day window. "Human label" is the team's manual triage category, used as a proxy — the honest caveat is that it is not blind adjudication. The qualification bench only ever runs on a copy of the database.
